Long-horizon LLM agents re-send a growing conversation history every step. A popular remedy, exemplified by SKILL.state, replaces that history with a compact, mutable, structured state object the model edits each step, promising lower cost at equal accuracy. We test this on Terminal-Bench 2.1 (89 tasks, DeepSeek V4 Flash) under a production prefix cache and find the promise fails: structured state cuts prompt tokens to about one quarter yet costs 30-70% more per step and is accuracy-neutral. A prefix cache makes already-seen tokens nearly free, so the real cost axis is uncached re-encodes plus output tokens; rewriting a mutable block every step collapses the cache hit rate from 97% to 49% and emits extra output, both hidden by the token count. We derive a closed-form break-even: structured state is cheaper only when the cache-hit price exceeds a threshold r* of the miss price (r* = 0.155-0.189 on our workload). Every major provider now prices its cache hit at r <= 0.10 (DeepSeek 0.02 after a cut; OpenAI, Gemini, Anthropic 0.10), all below r*, so append-only is cost-optimal everywhere today. A length-driven crossover near 131k tokens exists at r = 0.10 but vanishes at DeepSeek's r = 0.02. A generality check on Gemini 3.5 Flash exposes a third input, cache reliability: its best-effort implicit cache realizes only 89% of its nominal hit rate, lifting the effective discount to 0.195, within 0.006 of r*; the law predicts a knife-edge and the paired data show one. On SWE-bench Verified the main result reproduces exactly (identical accuracy, 2.1x cost). A position-independent KV cache built on an IBM vLLM fork (MiniPIC) gives no cost gain and destroys output quality. We conclude with a two-axis law: under prefix caching, compression is justified only by the space constraint of a finite context window, never by token price; when history fits the window, append only.
